# Making Internal Guidance Work Better for staff

**How identifying a recurring information problem helped reduce unnecessary enquiries and improve operational efficiency.**

**Service Improvement | User Experience | Stakeholder Management | Project Delivery**

**Role:** Joint project ownership with a colleague  
**Project period:** 2026, with an early September delivery target  
**Outcome:** Updated guidance and an observed reduction in related enquiries

---

## The Challenge

**Staff were repeatedly asking our team for information that should have been accessible through existing internal guidance.**

Although the information was maintained by other teams, our mailbox regularly received enquiries about it.

We would direct staff to the appropriate channels, but similar enquiries continued.

Over time, this became more than an inconvenience. Responding to recurring questions was creating additional work and slowing the processes our team was responsible for delivering.

The existing approach was reactive. We were repeatedly managing the consequences of an information problem without addressing its underlying cause.

### The problem we needed to solve

**How might we help staff find the information they need independently while reducing avoidable enquiries to our team?**

We needed a solution that would improve the employee experience without compromising the accuracy of information maintained by other teams.

---

## 01 / Recognising the Underlying Problem

The starting point was an operational pattern we had observed through everyday work.

Staff continued raising similar questions, even when we directed them to the appropriate sources of information.

This suggested that simply redirecting people was not effectively resolving the issue.

Instead of treating each enquiry as an isolated request, we recognised an opportunity to improve the guidance staff were expected to rely on.

### What we knew

| Observation | Why it mattered |
|---|---|
| Similar enquiries were arriving repeatedly | Staff were continuing to seek assistance |
| Information was already supposed to be available | Existing guidance was not sufficiently resolving their needs |
| Responsibility sat with other teams | Any improvement required cross-team coordination |
| Enquiries were slowing our own processes | The problem had an operational cost beyond staff frustration |

**Our key insight:** Improving access to useful information could be more effective than continuing to redirect staff individually.

This was based on recurring operational observations, rather than a formal user research study.

---

## 02 / Taking Ownership

Although maintaining the intranet guidance was not originally our team's responsibility, a colleague and I decided to take a more proactive approach.

We recognised that waiting for enquiries to stop was unlikely to resolve the problem.

Rather than continue handling the same requests, we took responsibility for coordinating an improvement to the existing information.

This decision meant working beyond our team's immediate remit while respecting the responsibilities of the teams that owned the guidance.

### Our approach

**Address the source of the problem, not just its symptoms.**

We decided to:

1. Coordinate improvements to the existing guidance.
2. Engage the responsible teams to ensure appropriate input and accuracy.
3. Share the delivery work between us.
4. Work towards an agreed deadline in early September.

The objective was not to create an entirely new service. It was to make an existing one more effective for the staff using it.

---

## 03 / Coordinating Across Teams

One of the key challenges was that the guidance was maintained outside our team.

Although the recurring enquiries were affecting our work, we could not treat the information as something we independently owned.

We therefore followed up with the relevant teams to coordinate the updates.

Their involvement was important because the guidance needed to remain consistent with the information and responsibilities they managed.

### Balancing initiative with governance

<escape>**Our team**</escape>  
Identified the operational problem and proactively coordinated the improvement.

<escape>**Responsible stakeholders**</escape>  
Provided input relating to the guidance within their remit.

<escape>**Senior approval**</escape>  
Was obtained before the revised information was published.

This approach allowed us to move the improvement forward without bypassing the appropriate stakeholders.

---

## 04 / Planning and Delivering the Improvement

Because we were completing this work alongside our existing responsibilities, we needed a practical delivery plan.

My colleague and I divided the work between us and agreed on a target of early September.

Splitting responsibilities helped us coordinate the different activities while keeping the project moving.

### Delivery journey

| Stage | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Identify** | Recognised recurring enquiries and their impact on operational efficiency |
| **Decide** | Chose to address the guidance rather than continue relying on redirection |
| **Coordinate** | Engaged the teams responsible for the information |
| **Plan** | Divided responsibilities and established an early September deadline |
| **Deliver** | Worked together to improve the existing guidance |
| **Approve** | Obtained the necessary senior approval before publication |
| **Observe** | Noticed fewer related enquiries after the updated guidance became available |

The improvement was delivered through collaboration rather than through a formal product development team.

Nevertheless, the work required several decisions common to product delivery: identifying a user problem, defining an improvement, coordinating stakeholders and managing work towards an outcome.

---

## 05 / The Outcome

**Following publication of the updated guidance, our team observed fewer related enquiries.**

This was particularly meaningful because reducing these enquiries was one of the reasons we had initiated the project.

<escape>### Before</escape>

Staff repeatedly contacted our team about guidance maintained elsewhere, creating additional correspondence and slowing existing processes.

<escape>### After</escape>

Revised guidance was available, and our team observed a reduction in the related enquiries it received.

### What the result tells us

The reduction was consistent with the intended outcome of making the guidance more useful and reducing avoidable contact.

However, we did not establish a quantified measure of the change, so I would not attribute a precise percentage improvement to the project.

The result is therefore presented as an **observed operational improvement**, not a statistically measured reduction.

---

## 06 / Reflection

### What I learned

**Taking ownership does not always mean owning the underlying service.**

One of the most valuable aspects of this project was recognising that a problem affecting our team could be addressed by improving something maintained elsewhere.

Rather than allowing organisational boundaries to prevent progress, we worked with the responsible stakeholders to deliver a solution.

The experience reinforced the importance of understanding the wider impact of recurring user problems, taking initiative and coordinating delivery across teams.

### What I would do differently

If approaching a similar project again, I would establish a clearer way to evaluate its impact from the beginning.

For example, recording the volume of relevant enquiries before and after publication would make it easier to quantify the improvement and assess whether further changes were needed.

I would also consider gathering structured feedback from staff to understand whether the revised guidance answered their questions more effectively.

These are opportunities I identified for future improvement, rather than activities undertaken during the original project.

---

## Final Reflection

This project began with a recurring operational frustration: staff were asking questions that existing guidance should have helped them answer.

By recognising the wider problem, taking initiative and working collaboratively across teams, we helped improve an existing internal service.

**The most important lesson was that meaningful service improvements do not always require building something new. Sometimes they begin with recognising that an existing experience is not working as intended and taking responsibility for improving it.**
