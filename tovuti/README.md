## 01 / The Product

### What was Tovuti?

Tovuti was a lifestyle and entertainment website that I independently conceived, designed and built using Wix.

The idea was to create a digital space where people could enjoy themselves while discovering something interesting or useful.

Rather than focusing exclusively on education, entertainment or news, I wanted to combine different types of content within one platform.

Visitors could explore lifestyle articles, read practical advice, discover information, listen to music, engage with a quiz or poll, and explore challenges relating to their wellbeing.

The platform was deliberately informal and varied. It was designed to offer a mixture of entertainment, information and opportunities for participation.

**My objective was to turn a personal creative idea into a functioning digital experience that visitors could explore and interact with.**

### Product at a glance

| Area | Description |
|---|---|
| Product | Lifestyle, entertainment and information website |
| Audience | General readers, including students and young people |
| Platform | Wix |
| Main experiences | Articles, news, music, quiz/poll, lifestyle challenges |
| Communication | Contact form and newsletter subscription |
| Product evaluation | Informal user testing, visitor feedback and historical analytics |

## 02 / Home — Creating the First Impression

The homepage introduced visitors to the Tovuti brand and provided access to the different sections of the platform.

I wanted it to communicate the informal personality of the website while providing visitors with different ways to explore its content.

### Entertainment and interaction

The homepage incorporated a playlist of songs, allowing visitors to discover music as part of the experience.

I also included a quiz or poll to introduce a more playful element.

These features reflected the original intention behind Tovuti: it was meant to be an enjoyable lifestyle destination, not simply somewhere people went to read articles.

The website also featured content discovery and navigation elements to help visitors move between its different sections.

### Product thinking

By combining entertainment with information, I was experimenting with how different features could contribute to the overall visitor experience.

The playlist gave visitors something to enjoy, while the interactive question offered a reason to participate rather than passively browse.

I wanted people to feel that there was more to explore than a conventional blog.

## 03 / Posts — Combining Education and Lifestyle Content

An important part of Tovuti was its collection of articles.

I created content covering a variety of subjects, bringing together practical advice, educational resources, personal experiences and lifestyle recommendations.

### Content areas

Examples of articles included:

**Education and student resources**
- Top 10 GCSE Revision Sites 2020
- Top 10 Grammar Checker Tool
- List of 5 Best Student Discount Sites

**Practical advice and personal experiences**
- How I Passed My Driving Theory
- Top 10 Ways to Make Money Online
- 3 Self-Improvement Tips

**Lifestyle and general interests**
- Top 10 Superfoods
- 6 Must-Have Apps for Your Phone
- Top 3 Recycling Tips

The variety was intentional.

I wanted visitors to be able to discover useful information without restricting the website to a single subject.

### Organising the content

I created a dedicated Posts page where visitors could browse the available articles.

The page included category navigation, allowing posts to be explored using different numbered-list formats.

This helped give the content a recognisable structure while supporting browsing and discovery.

### Product thinking

The Posts section reflected a decision to organise a broad range of information within a single content experience.

Rather than placing everything directly on the homepage, a dedicated section gave visitors a clearer place to explore articles.

The challenge was balancing variety with organisation so that the platform remained understandable as it expanded.

## 05 / About — Developing the Brand

### From TOP TEN to Tovuti

My original plan was to call the website **TOP TEN**.

At the time, much of the content was based around numbered lists of recommendations and resources.

However, I discovered that another website was already using the domain name I wanted.

This led me to reconsider the brand.

I eventually selected **Tovuti**, a Swahili word meaning *website*.

### An unexpected benefit

Although the initial reason for changing the name was practical, I recognised that the new identity gave the website greater flexibility.

The name TOP TEN suggested that the content would primarily consist of top-ten lists.

Tovuti was less restrictive.

It allowed me to explore other subjects, formats and experiences without creating a mismatch between the brand and its content.

| Original idea | Revised direction |
|---|---|
| TOP TEN | TOVUTI |
| Primarily numbered lists | Broader lifestyle platform |
| Content-led identity | Flexible identity accommodating different experiences |
| Narrower original concept | Entertainment, information and interaction |

**Key product decision:** I adapted the identity of the platform when the original concept became limiting, rather than continuing with a name that no longer suited its direction.

This was one of my earliest experiences of making a decision that affected the positioning of a digital product.

## 06 / Challenges — Encouraging Participation

I introduced a dedicated Challenges page to extend Tovuti beyond music and informational content.

The section offered activities relating to physical health, wellbeing and personal development.

### Challenges featured

| Challenge | Duration |
|---|---|
| The Ultimate Sugar Reset | 8 weeks |
| Get Running! | 30 days |
| Learn to Meditate | 3 weeks |

Each challenge appeared as an individual card containing an image, title, duration and a button to view further information.

The aim was to give visitors activities they could explore rather than limit their experience to reading and listening.

### Product thinking

The Challenges section represented an experiment with a different type of website engagement.

While articles delivered information and the playlist offered entertainment, challenges encouraged visitors to consider taking action.

This broadened the types of experiences available within the platform.

The section also required consideration of how activities should be presented.

Using separate cards made the challenges visually distinct and allowed visitors to compare the options available.

### Visitor communication

The website's messaging functionality enabled me to receive responses from people who had interacted with Tovuti.

I retained examples of these messages from 2020, including feedback about the website's presentation and content.

One visitor complimented the website's name and appearance.

Another described the website as informative.

Although these messages were informal, they provided direct feedback from people who had experienced the product.

### Product thinking

The Contact section created a basic feedback channel.

It gave visitors an opportunity to communicate their impressions, while allowing me to hear perspectives beyond my own.

This contributed to my understanding that developing a website involves more than designing its interface; it also involves considering how people respond to it.

## 08 / User Testing Before Launch

One of the decisions I made during development was to select people to test Tovuti before launching it.

I wanted to check that the website functioned as expected and gather feedback about the experience.

### My approach

| Stage | Action |
|---|---|
| Development | Created the initial website using Wix |
| Participant selection | Selected individuals to test the website |
| Testing | Invited people to explore the website and check its functionality |
| Feedback | Gathered responses about their experience |

This was an informal testing process rather than a formal usability study.

Nevertheless, it introduced an external perspective into the project.

As the person building Tovuti, I already understood where everything was located and how I expected the website to behave.

Inviting other people to test it helped me consider the experience from the visitor's point of view.

### Historical feedback

Archived messages from June 2020 demonstrate that people interacted with the website and shared positive impressions.

The feedback I retained included comments about the website being informative and the appeal of its name and presentation.

These responses were encouraging, although they were not sufficient to establish whether every feature worked successfully or whether particular design changes improved usability.

**Key learning:** Feedback is valuable because it provides perspectives beyond the creator's assumptions, but a structured testing process is needed to turn feedback into measurable improvements.

### Visitor Feedback

Alongside the informal pre-launch testing, Tovuti received direct messages from visitors through its contact functionality.

The archived messages included positive feedback on the website's branding, presentation and informational content.

For example:

"Great use of your time. Like the name of the website perfectly improvised."

"The website was very informative!!"

These messages provided qualitative evidence of how visitors responded to the platform.

While encouraging, they were informal reactions rather than structured usability findings, and I cannot attribute specific product improvements to them.

Key learning: Direct visitor feedback can help identify what people appreciate about a product, while more structured testing is needed to understand usability problems and opportunities for improvement.

## 09 / Analysing Early Engagement

I used Wix's built-in analytics to understand how visitors interacted with Tovuti during its initial period of activity.

### Website Traffic Overview

Between **31 January and 31 July 2020**, Wix recorded:

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Website sessions | 222 |
| Unique visitors | 160 |
| New visitors | 158 (99%) |
| Returning visitors | 2 (1%) |
| Mobile device share | 60% |
| Desktop device share | 40% |

The figures showed that Tovuti attracted visitors beyond the people involved in its initial testing.

Most recorded visitors were new, suggesting that the website's early activity was primarily driven by initial discovery rather than repeat engagement.

The device breakdown also showed that mobile accounted for the majority of classified sessions, highlighting the importance of considering how the platform appeared and functioned on smaller screens.

### What I Learned

These analytics provided an early introduction to evaluating a digital product using actual visitor behaviour.

The traffic report demonstrated that Tovuti was attracting visitors, while the blog data showed that different content topics generated different levels of interest.

However, the high proportion of new visitors and limited evidence of returning users also highlighted an important distinction between initial reach and sustained engagement.

If I were developing Tovuti today, I would prioritise investigating what encouraged visitors to return, how successfully mobile users navigated the website and which experiences provided the greatest value.

## 10 / Product Decisions and Trade-offs

Developing Tovuti required me to make decisions about its identity, content, functionality and presentation.

| Decision | Reasoning | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Develop a lifestyle platform | Wanted to combine entertainment with useful information | Created a website spanning multiple areas of interest |
| Change TOP TEN to Tovuti | The original domain was unavailable and the name limited content positioning | Developed a more flexible brand identity |
| Use Wix | Needed an accessible way to build and publish the website | Created a functioning digital platform without building its underlying infrastructure |
| Include a playlist and quiz/poll | Wanted visitors to have fun as well as read content | Introduced entertainment and lightweight interaction |
| Organise articles into a Posts section | Needed a dedicated space for different content topics | Created a browsable content library |
| Add challenges | Wanted to explore interaction beyond reading articles | Introduced activities relating to wellbeing |
| Include contact functionality | Wanted visitors to be able to communicate directly | Established a visitor feedback channel |
| Test before launch | Wanted perspectives beyond my own | Collected early feedback |
| Review Wix analytics | Wanted evidence of content engagement | Identified variation in article performance |

These decisions were made as part of an independent creative project rather than within a professional product team.

However, they provided early exposure to decisions that also matter in product management: defining a concept, developing an experience, considering users and interpreting evidence.

## 11 / Retrospective Evaluation

### What worked

**Turning an idea into something tangible**

I moved from wanting to create a website to building and publishing a digital platform with multiple sections and interactive elements.

**Creating a distinctive experience**

Tovuti combined music, interactive questions, educational content, news and lifestyle activities rather than focusing on a single format.

**Adapting the product's identity**

Changing the name from TOP TEN to Tovuti allowed me to broaden the concept beyond numbered articles.

**Involving users**

Inviting people to test the website before launch introduced the importance of receiving feedback from outside the development process.

**Examining engagement**

Wix analytics provided early evidence that visitors were accessing and interacting with published content.

### What I would improve today

Looking back with a stronger understanding of product development, I would approach several parts of the project differently.

**1. Define the target audience more clearly**

Tovuti covered a broad range of interests. I would begin by identifying the audience I wanted to serve and understanding which experiences mattered most to them.

**2. Clarify the central product proposition**

The combination of entertainment, education and news created variety, but I would investigate which features gave users the strongest reason to visit.

**3. Conduct structured usability testing**

I would create specific tasks for testers, record where they encountered difficulties and assess whether the experience supported successful task completion.

**4. Prioritise features using evidence**

Instead of expanding the website based mainly on personal interest, I would evaluate which features supported the platform's purpose and users' needs.

**5. Define clearer success metrics**

Alongside article views, I would consider returning visitors, navigation success, newsletter subscriptions and engagement with interactive features.

**6. Document iteration**

I would maintain a record of user feedback, product decisions, improvements and the evidence behind them.

These are improvements I would make today, not activities I am claiming to have completed in 2020.

---

## 12 / Final Reflection

Tovuti was an early experience of independently conceiving, building and testing a digital product.

I created it because I wanted to make something enjoyable: a place where people could listen to music, discover interesting information, learn useful things and explore activities.

As the project developed, I gained experience making decisions about branding, content organisation, visitor interaction and website functionality.

I also learned the value of gathering external feedback and examining engagement data rather than relying entirely on my own impressions.

Although Tovuti was a relatively small, independently developed project, it helped introduce me to the broader considerations involved in creating digital experiences.

**My biggest takeaway was that a digital product is more than a collection of features. Its value depends on the experience those features create for the people using it.**
